# Appearance: そう / みたい

Learn how to describe how things look, seem, or appear using **そう** and **みたい**.

## Key Expressions

| Hiragana | Romaji | English | Type |
|----------|--------|---------|------|
| [stem] + そう | [stem] sou | looks like, appears to be | expression |
| [plain] + みたい | [plain] mitai | seems like, looks like | expression |

:::tip When to Use Which?
- **そう** (*sou*) — Visual impression based directly on what you observe right in front of you ("it looks...", "appears...").
- **みたい** (*mitai*) — Resemblance, intuitive guess, or indirect inference ("it seems like...", "looks like...").
:::

## Pattern 1: そう (Visual Impression)

**[verb stem / adjective stem] + そう (です)**

**Meaning:** "Looks like...", "seems like...", "appears to be..."

### Formation

| Type | Formation | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Verb | [verb stem] + そう | ふりそう (looks like it will rain) |
| い-Adj | [い-adj without い] + そう | おいしそう (looks delicious) |
| な-Adj | [な-adj without な] + そう | げんきそう (looks energetic / healthy) |

- Attach to the **verb stem** (the ます stem) to express that an event looks imminent or about to happen.
- Drop the final **い** from **い-adjectives** (e.g. おいしい → おいしそう).
- Drop **な** from **な-adjectives** (e.g. げんきな → げんきそう).
- **Note:** **そう** cannot attach directly to nouns for appearance (for nouns, use **みたい**).

:::warning Inherent Visual Adjectives: かわいい, かっこいい, こわい
Adjectives that already inherently describe an obvious visual appearance or direct sensory impression are **not** used with **〜そう**. Because their quality is self-evident at a glance, you use the adjective directly or pair it with **みたい**:
- **かわいい** (*cute*): Say **かわいい です** or **かわいい みたい です**, NEVER ~~かわいそう です~~.
- **かわいそう (*kawaisou*) is a false friend**: **かわいそう** is a separate word meaning **"pitiful"**, **"poor thing"**, or **"pathetic"** (e.g. かわいそう な いぬ — *a poor, pitiful dog*). It does **NOT** mean "looks cute"!
- **かっこいい** (*cool / good-looking*) & **きれい** (*pretty / clean*): Say **かっこいい です** or **きれい です** directly.
- **こわい** (*scary*): Typically expressed as **こわい です** directly or with **みたい** rather than ~~こわそう~~ when observing an obvious scary appearance.
:::

### Examples

- この ケーキ は おいしそう です。 — This cake looks delicious.
  - *kono keeki wa oishisou desu.*
- その かばん は たかそう です。 — That bag looks expensive.
  - *sono kaban wa takasou desu.*
- あの ひと は しんせつそう です。 — That person looks kind.
  - *ano hito wa shinsetsusou desu.*
- この こ は げんきそう です。 — This child looks healthy.
  - *kono ko wa genkisou desu.*
- いま に も あめ が ふりそう です。 — It looks like it is about to rain at any moment.
  - *ima ni mo ame ga furisou desu.*

:::caution Exceptions: いい and ない
Two adjectives form **そう** irregularly:
- **いい → よさそう** (not ~~いそう~~): この ほん は よさそう です (This book looks good).
  - *kono hon wa yosasou desu.*
- **ない → なさそう** (not ~~なそう~~): もんだい は なさそう です (There seems to be no problem).
  - *mondai wa nasasou desu.*
:::

### Negative: Doesn't Look...

To express that something does *not* look like a certain quality, make the adjective negative first before adding **そう**. Because the negative **ない** becomes **なさそう**, both い-adjectives and な-adjectives end in **〜なさそう**:

| Type | Negative Formation | Example |
|------|--------------------|---------|
| い-Adj | [い-adj without い] + くなさそう | おいしくなさそう (doesn't look tasty) |
| な-Adj | [な-adj] + じゃなさそう | げんき じゃなさそう (doesn't look healthy) |

- この りょうり は おいしくなさそう です。 — This dish doesn't look tasty.
  - *kono ryouri wa oishikunasasou desu.*
- あの ひと は げんき じゃなさそう です。 — That person doesn't look well.
  - *ano hito wa genki janasasou desu.*
- この えいが は おもしろくなさそう です。 — This movie doesn't look interesting.
  - *kono eiga wa omoshirokunasasou desu.*

## Pattern 2: みたい (Seems Like / Resemblance)

**[plain form / noun] + みたい (です)**

**Meaning:** "Seems like...", "looks like...", "resembles..."

### Formation

| Type | Formation | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Verb | [plain form] + みたい | ふる みたい (seems like it will rain) |
| い-Adj | [い-adj plain] + みたい | おいしい みたい (seems delicious) |
| な-Adj | [な-adj stem] + みたい | げんき みたい (seems energetic) |
| Noun | [noun] + みたい | がくせい みたい (looks like a student) |

- Attach directly to the **plain form** of verbs and い-adjectives.
- Attach directly to **nouns** and the stem of **な-adjectives** (drop だ: e.g. げんき みたい, がくせい みたい).

### Examples

- あめ が ふる みたい です。 — It looks like it will rain.
  - *ame ga furu mitai desu.*
- あの ひと は がくせい みたい です。 — That person looks like a student.
  - *gakusei mitai desu.*
- この りんご は おいしい みたい です。 — It seems this apple is delicious.
  - *kono ringo wa oishii mitai desu.*
- たなかさん は いそがしい みたい です。 — Tanaka-san seems busy.
  - *tanaka-san wa isogashii mitai desu.*
- かれ は にほんじん みたい に にほんご を はなします。 — He speaks Japanese like a native Japanese person.
  - *kare wa nihonjin mitai ni nihongo o hanashimasu.*

### Negative: Doesn't Seem Like

To form the negative, make the word or clause **before** みたい negative. **みたい です** itself does not change:

| Type | Negative Formation | Example |
|------|--------------------|---------|
| Verb | [plain negative] + みたい | ふらない みたい (seems like it won't rain) |
| い-Adj | [〜くない] + みたい | おいしくない みたい (doesn't seem delicious) |
| な-Adj | [〜じゃない] + みたい | げんき じゃない みたい (doesn't seem energetic) |
| Noun | [noun] + じゃない + みたい | がくせい じゃない みたい (doesn't seem like a student) |

- あめ が ふらない みたい です。 — It looks like it won't rain.
  - *ame ga furanai mitai desu.*
- あの ひと は がくせい じゃない みたい です。 — That person doesn't seem to be a student.
  - *ano hito wa gakusei janai mitai desu.*
- この りょうり は おいしくない みたい です。 — This food doesn't seem delicious.
  - *kono ryouri wa oishikunai mitai desu.*

## Same Sentence, Both Ways

**そう (direct visual observation):**
- あめ が ふりそう です。 — It looks like it is about to rain.
  - *ame ga furisou desu.*
*(Looking up at dark, heavy storm clouds right now.)*

**みたい (indirect inference / external evidence):**
- あめ が ふる みたい です。 — It seems like it will rain.
  - *ame ga furu mitai desu.*
*(Saw the weather forecast, or saw people on the street carrying wet umbrellas.)*

## Quick Comparison

**そう** — Expresses an impression formed from immediate, direct visual evidence (what something looks like right now, or an action about to happen). Cannot attach directly to nouns for appearance.

**みたい** — Expresses conjecture, resemblance, or general likeness based on indirect clues, sensory input, or hearsay. Attaches directly to nouns, verbs, and adjectives.

**Key difference:** Use **そう** when judging directly with your eyes ("looks delicious"), and **みたい** when making an inference or simile ("seems delicious based on what I heard/read", "acts like a child").

:::info Expressing Possibility
To say something *might* happen or *probably will* happen based on probability rather than appearance, see the [Expressing Possibility](/docs/lessons/grammar/feelings-and-intent/possibility) article (かもしれない / でしょう).
:::
